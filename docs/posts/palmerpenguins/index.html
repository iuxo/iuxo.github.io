When working with new data its a good idea to get a feel for the data, what do rows and columns look like, what does a data entry look like.

First import the necessary libraries

import numpy as np

After taking a look, I want to again see the distribution of penguins using sex and species.

# 2. Grouped Bar Chart: Species distribution broken down by Sex
fig_bar = px.histogram(
    penguins.assign(sex=penguins["sex"].fillna("Missing")),
    x="species",
    color="sex",
    barmode="group",
    title="Palmer Penguins: Distribution of Species by Sex",
    labels={"species": "Penguin Species", "count": "Count", "sex": "Sex"},
    color_discrete_map={
        "male": "#3366CC",
        "female": "#FF6699",
        "Missing": "#B6B6B6",
    },
    text_auto=True,
)
fig_bar.update_layout(
    xaxis_title="Species",
    yaxis_title="Number of Penguins",
    legend_title_text="Sex",
    template="plotly_white",
)
fig_bar.show()

Distribution of Penguins by Species

# 3. Donut Charts: Species and Sex Proportions
fig_species_pie = px.pie(
    penguins,
    names="species",
    title="Species Proportion Breakdown",
    hole=0.4,
    color_discrete_sequence=px.colors.qualitative.Set2,
)
fig_species_pie.show()

Proportion of Penguins by Species

fig_sex_pie = px.pie(
    penguins.dropna(subset=["sex"]),
    names="sex",
    title="Sex Breakdown (Excluding Missing)",
    hole=0.4,
    color_discrete_map={"male": "#3366CC", "female": "#FF6699"},
)
fig_sex_pie.show()

Proportion of Penguins by Sex

We can see that there is a lot less data on the Chinstrap species, either it is less common and thus we have less information, or the way we have gathered data, we have just gathered less on the Chinstrap compared to Adelie and Gentoo. The data represents the male and female penguins evenly which is good.
